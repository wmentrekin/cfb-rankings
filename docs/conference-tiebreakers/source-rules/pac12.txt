PAC-12 CONFERENCE — FOOTBALL CHAMPIONSHIP GAME TIEBREAKERS
Supplied by repo owner 2026-09-12. Explicitly the 2026 policy for the rebuilt conference.

CONTEXT FROM THE SAME SOURCE:
The 2026 Pac-12 Football Championship Game will be played Friday, Dec. 4, hosted at the home
venue of the league's highest seed. The matchup will feature the top two eligible teams in the
final regular-season standings, with host and seeding determined by the tiebreaking procedures.

For the 2026 season the Pac-12 will play a SEVEN-GAME conference schedule over an eight-week
span from weeks five through 12, with each program receiving one bye over that stretch. THE
FINAL WEEK OF THE REGULAR SEASON, WEEK 13, WILL FEATURE A NEW HOME-AND-HOME FLEX MATCHUP THAT
WILL NOT COUNT TOWARD CONFERENCE STANDINGS.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
TWO-TEAM TIES
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. If two teams are tied for first place both teams will participate in the championship game
   and the winner of the head-to-head will be the No. 1 seed. If there is not a tie for first
   place, but two teams are tied for second place, the two-team tiebreaker policy will apply and
   the winner will be the No. 2 seed and visiting team.
2. Win percentage against the next-highest-placed common opponent in the standings (based on
   record in all games played within the Conference), proceeding through the standings.
   - When arriving at another group of tied teams while comparing records, use each team's win
     percentage against the collective tied teams as a group (prior to that group's own
     tiebreaking procedure) rather than the performance against individual tied teams.
3. Win percentage against all common conference opponents.
4. Highest CFP ranking(s) on the Tuesday prior to week 13 contests.
5. Highest ranking by SportSource Analytics (team Rating Score metric) following the last
   weekend of regular-season conference games.
6. Coin toss

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
MULTIPLE-TEAM TIES
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
In the event of a tie involving more than two teams, the Pac-12 will use a progressive
multiple-team tiebreaking procedure to establish championship game participants and seeding.

Once one team gains an advantage and is seeded, the remaining teams will restart the applicable
multiple-team tiebreaking procedure. If the process reduces the tie to two teams, the two-team
procedure will then be applied.

1. Head-to-head results, based on the best cumulative winning percentage among the tied teams.
   IF EVERY TIED TEAM HAS NOT PLAYED ONE ANOTHER, THE PROCESS MOVES TO THE NEXT CRITERION.
2. Winning percentage against all common Conference opponents.
3. Record against the next-highest placed common opponent in the Conference standings,
   proceeding through the standings as necessary.
   - When arriving at another group of tied teams, each team's winning percentage against the
     collective tied group will be used before that group's own tiebreaking procedure is applied.
4. Combined Conference winning percentage of Conference opponents, measuring strength of
   Conference schedule.
5. Highest ranking in the SportSource Analytics Team Rating Score following the final weekend of
   regular-season games.
6. Coin toss.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
IMPLEMENTATION NOTES (added by the implementer, not part of the source)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
- SEASON SCOPE IS CRITICAL. This is the 2026 policy for an eight-team conference. In 2024 and
  2025 the Pac-12 consisted of Oregon State and Washington State ALONE -- two members and no
  championship game -- so this document must NOT be applied to those seasons.
- The two-team chain is the FIRST supplied procedure that puts record-against-placed-opponents
  (step 2) BEFORE record-against-all-common-opponents (step 3). The SEC, Big 12, Big Ten, MAC
  and CUSA all have those two the other way round. The multi-team chain reverts to the common
  order. This is the clearest single vindication of keeping step order as data.
- Its multi-team step 1 has NO sweep clause of any kind: if the tied teams did not all play one
  another the step is simply void. The Mountain West and American keep a "unless one team
  defeated all others" escape; the SEC and ACC promote AND demote. Three distinct readings.
- Two DIFFERENT external rankings appear in the two-team chain (CFP at 4, SportSource at 5).
  Substituting this project's own rating for both collapses them into a duplicate step.
- THE WEEK-13 FLEX GAMES DO NOT COUNT TOWARD CONFERENCE STANDINGS. artifacts/schedule.py's
  FLEX_WEEK_TBD_CONFIG already knows about this week and ASSUMES those games "will arrive as
  conference_game=false". That assumption is still unverified -- week 13 is unpopulated -- and if
  CFBD marks them conference_game=true they would contaminate conference records AND every
  tiebreaker step that reads them.
